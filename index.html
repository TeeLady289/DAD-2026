How to play Dad

Two games — you pick in the Settings menu on the table: there's Dad, and there's Dad (Cut Throat). They play exactly the same except for one thing: Automatic Jack is Cut Throat only. Pick Dad and there is no Automatic Jack at all. Pick Dad (Cut Throat) and Automatic Jack is in the game. Everything else — bidding, Dads, Bella, bucking, first to 500 — is the same in both.

Players: pick 2, 3, or 4 players in the Settings menu. With 4 players, you and the player across from you are partners. With 2 or 3 players, everybody plays for themselves. Final hand sizes: 9 cards each with 2 players (the bid card is not picked up — it stays in the kitty with the leftover undealt cards and sits out), 8 each with 3 players, and 6 each with 4 players.

The deck: only 24 cards — the 9, 10, Jack, Queen, King, and Ace in every suit. That's the "9 on down" deck.

Who deals first — the draw: to start a game, every player randomly picks one card from the deck. The player with the highest face value card becomes the dealer for the first hand — face value runs 9 (low), 10, Jack, Queen, King, Ace (high); the suit doesn't matter. If two or more players tie for the highest card, just those players pick again until one card is highest. You'll see every picked card face up on the table right in front of the player who picked it, at their own seat in the field of play — not in a separate box — and the winner gets the white DEALER badge. From that first dealer, the deal then rotates clockwise, hand after hand (see below).

Dealing rotates clockwise: the deal moves one seat clockwise every hand — it never stays with the same dealer, and a white DEALER badge sits on whoever deals. Clockwise from you goes to Hank on your left — going from you to Rosa on your right would be counter-clockwise. With 4 players the order around the table is You → Hank → Pearl → Rosa → back to You (with 3 players it's You → Hank → Pearl; with 2 it just goes back and forth between you and Pearl). Whoever won the opening draw deals the first hand, and the rotation runs clockwise from them. Cards are dealt clockwise too, 3 at a time starting with the player clockwise from the dealer (see "The deal" below), and turns — bidding, the "Any Dads?" answers, and play — all move in that same clockwise order.

Cutting the cards — before every deal: after the deck is shuffled and before a single card is dealt, the player to the right of the dealer cuts the cards — that's the player counter-clockwise from the dealer, the last one the clockwise rotation came from. With 4 players that's: your deal → Rosa cuts (she's on your right); Hank's deal → you cut; Pearl's deal → Hank cuts; Rosa's deal → Pearl cuts. When the cut is yours, the game pauses and gives you the cut buttons — cut near the top, in the middle, deep, or let it pick a random spot — and the top part of the deck moves to the bottom before the deal starts. Hank, Pearl, and Rosa cut at a spot of their own choosing when it's theirs. Only after the cut does the dealer deal.

The deal — 6 cards before the bid, the rest after: the deal comes in two stages. Before any bidding, the dealer deals 6 cards to each player, 3 at a time — a batch of 3 to each player clockwise, then another batch of 3 — until everybody has 6. You bid on those 6 cards, not on your finished hand. Once bidding is complete and trump is decided, the rest of the deck comes out, and how depends on the number of players:

  • 3 players: the dealer picks up the bid card. Then the dealer deals the other players 2 cards each, 2 at a time. Then the dealer keeps the bottom card — the last card left in the stock. Everybody ends up with 8 cards — and after trump is set and that second deal is done, those 8 cards are where you'll find your Dads and your Bella, if you have any.
  • 2 players — the kitty: the bid card is not picked up by the dealer. Each player is dealt 3 more cards — totalling 9 cards each. The bid card stays in the kitty on the side of the table with the remaining undealt cards, and the whole kitty sits out during game play. You'll see the kitty pile on the table for the rest of the hand — but face down: once trump is called, the bid card itself is not shown during play.
  • 4 players: there is no second deal — the first deal of 3 at a time already dealt out all the cards, 6 to each player.

The cards from the second deal count just like the first 6 — they can make your Dads and your Bella, because the "Any Dads?" round only happens after they're dealt. So in a 3-player game, once trump is set and the second deal is done, it's your full 8 cards where you'll find your Dads and your Bella, if you have any; in a 2-player game, it's your 9.

Placing the bid card: the bid card can be placed at any time during the dealing — early, in the middle, or late — it is not always the dealer's last card. With 2 or 3 players it's turned up out of the deck during the deal and set aside on the table while you bid. With 4 players, where every card is dealt in the first deal, it's one of the dealer's own dealt cards turned face up. When you are the dealer, the deal pauses before bidding and you get the placing buttons: Place bid card randomly (a random spot in the deal), or pick a spot yourself — Early, Middle, or Late in the deal, with the exact card in that part of the deal still random. Hank, Pearl, and Rosa place theirs randomly when they deal. Whichever card is placed turns face up on the table for everybody to bid on.

Picking trump — two rounds of bidding: the dealer turns up a bid card during the deal — you'll see it face up on the table. Round 1 is on the bid card: going around the table, each player can take the bid card's suit as trump, or pass. If everybody passes, round 2 is based purely on the strength of each player's hand: going around again, each player can name any suit for trump except the bid card's suit — that suit is out — or pass. If round 2 passes all the way back to the dealer, the dealer cannot pass out — there is no passing the hand in. The dealer must pick trump, based on the strength of their hand (still not the bid card's suit). Whoever names trump, their side has to outscore everybody else that hand — and whoever calls trump leads (plays first) on the first trick.

The bid card — dealer picks it up with 3 or 4 players, the kitty with 2 — and trump only shows once it's chosen: the bid card's suit is not trump just because it's turned up — the table shows no trump suit at all until a suit is actually chosen. The bid card is only face up while bidding is live; once trump is called it is not shown during game play at all — the only suit displayed from then on is the trump suit. With 3 or 4 players, once the bid card is chosen (somebody takes it up in round 1), the dealer picks the bid card up into their hand for game play and it leaves the table. And if the bid card is not chosen — passed by all players in round 1 — the dealer still picks it up for game play once trump is set; the new trump, chosen in round 2 based on the strength of the players' hands, is the suit that gets displayed on the table — not the bid card's suit. With 2 players it's different: chosen or passed, the bid card is never picked up — it stays in the kitty on the side with the remaining undealt cards, face down in the kitty pile, and the newly picked trump is still the only suit that gets displayed.

Card sounds: shuffling, dealing, and cards played to a trick all have sounds. Turn them off any time in Settings → Sound.

Automatic Jack — Cut Throat only: in Dad (Cut Throat), if the turned-up bid card is any Jack, there's no bidding at all — whatever suit that Jack is becomes trump automatically, exactly the same as if it had been called, and the dealer is the maker — so the dealer leads first, just like a caller. In regular Dad there is no Automatic Jack: a Jack bid card is just a bid card, and round 1 bidding goes ahead on its suit like any other card.

Card strength: in the trump suit, the Jack is boss, then the 9 — that's the part that surprises everybody. In the other suits, Ace is high and 9 is low.

Playing: the player who called trump leads the first trick — in Cut Throat with Automatic Jack, that's the dealer. After that, whoever won the last trick leads the next one. You must follow the suit that was led if you can. If you can't follow suit, you have to cut with a trump if you have one — you can only play off (throw a card from another suit) when you have no trumps at all in your hand. And if trump is led, you have to try to beat it: if you hold a trump that beats the trump that was led — and any higher trump already played on that trick — you must play a beating trump. Only when none of your trumps can beat it can you play a lower trump. Win a trick by playing the strongest card of the led suit — or the strongest trump. The last trick is called the trick, and winning it is worth 10 extra points.

Arranging your hand: your cards are yours to put in any order you like. Drag a card sideways — with your finger or the mouse — and drop it where you want it, at any time: while bidding, while the table asks "Any Dads?", or while you play. You can also tap a card to select it and move it with the Left / Right arrow keys. Once you've arranged your hand, the game leaves your order alone — playing a card just takes that card out, and your new cards from the second deal land at the end, ready to arrange. A plain tap still plays a card on your turn; only a real drag moves one. Tap Sort cards, right under your hand, any time you want the game's own order back (trump first, then by suit).

Heaving — just a way to play, not a rule or a button: heaving isn't a required part of the game and there's no button for it — it's simply a strategy in how you choose your card. When you can't follow suit, instead of throwing away a nothing card, you can just play your highest point card — an Ace, a 10, a trump 9 — onto a trick that's already being won, so those points land in books that count. In 4-player partners play, players do that onto a trick their partner is winning, just to run up points fast. In 3-player play it's a choice of strategy: pile your point cards onto the other defender's winning trick to help them outscore the maker and buck them, especially when the maker has the lead or is close to winning. You never have to — you heave, or you don't, just by the card you tap in normal play. The regular rules still apply: if you're void and holding a trump you still have to cut with a trump, and if trump is led you still have to try to beat it.

Meld — bonus points, but never automatic: after trump is called and before anybody plays a card, the table stops and asks "Any Dads?" — the hand does not close on you while you answer. Each player, in turn, answers "No Dad" or states their level without naming the suit: a top card and a length (or a four of a kind), like "King 5" (100 pts), "King 4" (50 pts), "Queen 3" (20 pts), "Four Aces" (100 pts), or "Four Jacks" (200 pts). Every answer shows right on the table, at that player's own seat in the field of play — yours above your hand, Pearl's under her seat, Hank's and Rosa's under theirs — just like the cards in the opening draw. There is no separate answers box to scroll down to. When the round settles, the winning player's seat turns gold and lists every Dad they showed, right there at their seat.

Help — On your own, or Tutorial (computer assist): the Help setting in the Settings menu decides how much the computer does for you. On your own is the regular way to play: there is no computer-assisted Dad or Bella recognition at all. You spot your own Dads, state your own level in the "Any Dads?" round — the answer buttons stand on their own, with no explainer box eating up the table — and you call "Part" and "Rest" yourself with the call buttons as you play. State a level you don't actually hold and there's nothing to show if it comes to you; state lower than your best and only the level you stated competes. Pick Tutorial — computer assist and the help turns on for beginners: the game works out your best Dad claim for you, tags your Bella cards, prompts your "Part" and "Rest" calls before the card lands, and shows the beginner explainer hints. Those hints live in Tutorial only — they never take up space in a regular game.

Only the player with the highest Dad shows — and in teams play, their whole team shows. Whoever holds the highest Dad turns their meld face up. Everybody on the other side stays hidden — suit and all — and scores nothing. But if that winning player holds more than one Dad, they show them all: every Dad in their hand turns face up and every one of them counts — not just the winning one. Teams play (4 players) works the same way for the whole team: if either team member holds the winning Dad, BOTH team members show — every Dad in both partners' hands turns face up and every one of them counts, even the partner's smaller Dad. So you can win the show with a King 4 and your partner's Queen 3 counts right alongside it. In 2- and 3-player games there's no team, so only the winning player shows. The game checks every Dad you're holding for the win, too: hold the same level twice with one of them in trump, and the trump one is your winning Dad. A longer Dad outranks a shorter one no matter the top card (a King 4 beats an Ace 3, even an Ace 3 in trump), and between Dads of the same length the higher top card wins. On an exact tie — same length, same top card — a Dad in trump beats a plain-suit one. Equal top Dads in plain suits on opposing sides cancel out — nobody shows that Dad.

  • Four Jacks — 200 points. The biggest meld in the game — Four Jacks is a Dad, and the highest Dad there is: no Dad can beat it or cancel it. So if you hold Four Jacks, you win the show outright and every Dad in your hand shows and counts right alongside it.
  • Four of a kind — 100 points: four 9s, four 10s, four Queens, four Kings, or four Aces.
  • A "Dad" — 20 points: three cards in a row in the same suit, like 9-10-Jack or Queen-King-Ace. A Dad is always worth its 20, even when the King and Queen are in it. If that King and Queen are not trump, the Dad is just its 20 — no Bella and nothing extra. Bella is only the King & Queen of trump, both held in one player's hand, and even then it is scored separately (see Bella below), never added into the Dad's own value.
  • Four in a row — 50 points.
  • Five or more in a row — 100 points. A long Dad: five cards in a row in one suit — "King 5" (9-10-J-Q-K) or "Ace 5" (10-J-Q-K-A) — is worth 100. So is the whole suit in one hand, "Ace 6" (9-10-J-Q-K-A): one Dad, one score of 100, not split into smaller Dads.

Four of a kind counts as your claim too: state it in the same "Any Dads?" round — "Four Aces" for 100, "Four Jacks" for 200 — and it outranks any run. And it all shows together: if the player who wins the show holds more than one Dad, or other meld besides the Dad they claimed, all of it turns face up and all of it scores together. In teams play that goes for your partner too — if your team wins the show, your partner's four of a kind and Dads all show and score with yours.

Bella — 20 points: the King and Queen of trump only — a King and Queen of any other suit is not a Bella, and a King and Queen split between two players is not a Bella either — one player has to hold both cards in hand to have it. Bella is its own thing, scored separately from any dad: a dad can get beaten and the Bella is still there to be won. But Bella is never automatic: you have to call "Part" out loud as you play the first of them, and "Rest" as you play the second. Forget a call — easy to do right after your dad just got beat — and the Bella scores nothing. Nothing stops you playing the King or Queen of trump without calling, though: the card plays exactly as normal, and you simply lose the 20 Bella points that the calls would have earned. And a King and Queen of a non-trump suit is never a Bella at all — no "Part", no "Rest", no points, even if they're held together in the same hand or sit inside a plain-suit Dad. Part and Rest stay right on the board while you play: a tracker strip on the felt, just above your cards, records every Part and Rest call the moment it's made. In Tutorial (computer assist) it also shows your Bella and the state of both calls the whole hand — still to call, "Part" called with "Rest" to come, both called, or lost — your King and Queen of trump carry a gold BELLA tag so you can spot them in your hand, and the game asks you before the card lands. On your own, none of that spotting happens: you watch your own cards, and you make the calls yourself with the Call "Part" / Call "Rest" buttons — tap the call, then play the card. When Hank, Pearl, or Rosa calls Part or Rest, it shows on that same strip the moment they play the card.

Scoring: after all the tricks, each side adds up its card points, meld, and the last-trick bonus. If the side that called trump outscored everybody else, each side keeps its own points. If they didn't, that's a buck — see below.

Bucking: calling trump and not making it is a buck. The side that got bucked scores 0 for the hand and loses extra points on top of that: −50 in a 2- or 3-player game, −100 with 4 players. When a hand ends, its results flash in the middle of the table for about 15 seconds while the deck is shuffled, then the next hand deals itself — and the buck shows right in the score too: the bucked team or player is marked BUCKED on their score card, on the table.

You can come back from a buck. A buck does not end the match and nobody is knocked out. Scores are never stopped at zero — a buck can put a side in the negatives, and that's fine: they just have further to climb. That's what makes the score fluctuate — one hand you're up, the next you're bucked back down, and later in the same match you can buck the other side right back. Each team may buck the other throughout the match. The game keeps dealing, hand after hand, until somebody actually reaches 500. Down is not out — not in Dad.

Card designs: win hands to earn new card designs — fresh colors for the card backs. Win a whole game to earn the Champion Gold design. Pick any design you've earned right here in the Settings menu, under Card designs. Ocean Swirl is our own original design — deep-blue ocean swirls with a spade at the centre and pale-blue faces — ready to pick from the start.

Table: pick the table you play on in Settings → Table. There are three real-looking tables, all ready from the start — no winning needed: Green Felt, the classic casino table; Dark Wood, a smooth peanut-butter top with a polished wood border and no felt; and Purple Felt, rich purple casino felt. Switching tables never restarts your game — the new table is just there, even in the middle of a hand — and your card design stays whatever you picked.

Play-chip tables (Chips setting — pick it before you start): these are play chips only — no real money, no cash value, and chips can't be cashed out. Pick a $1, $2, or $5 table in the Settings menu (or leave chips Off for points only). Everybody starts with 100 chips. Each hand, every player antes the table stake into the main pot, and the side that wins the hand takes it (partners split it). On the first hand only, everybody antes the stake again into a side pot: it's judged on each player's first 6 cards as a poker hand — pair, two pair, three of a kind, straight, flush, full house, four of a kind — and the best poker hand wins it, paid when the hand ends (a tie splits it). The stakes, both pots, and everybody's stack sit on the table under the scores. If a stack can't cover the ante, the table offers a rebuy that tops it back up to 100 — stacks never go negative.
